62% of a Desi Plate Is Carbs. Here's What's Actually Recommended.

Lose fat. Keep the roti.

Sponsor this newsletter — email hanzalafar29@gmail.com

Salaam, namaste, hello 👋

We grew up on roti, chawal, daal and chai. Nobody's quitting that. But do you know how many carbs that actually adds up to in a week, and what the guidelines say?

I pulled the research and ran the numbers. Here's the quick version. 👇

The big number Example day Recommended Roti, naan, rice Chai Swaps Myth vs fact Recipe Sources

📊 The big number

62%

of Indian adults' daily calories come from carbs.

ICMR–INDIAB survey of 18,090 adults, Nature Medicine 2025

What that means

  • Health Canada's recommended range for adults is 45–65% of calories from carbs. So 62% sits near the top of the range.
  • The bigger issue is what kind of carbs. The largest share comes from refined cereals like white rice and maida (28.5% of calories), then milled whole grains like atta (16.2%).
  • Protein is only 12% of calories, below India's own guideline of 15%.
  • In the diaspora it's a bit lower. South Asians living in the US got 55–57% of calories from carbs, about 240–251 g a day (MASALA study).

✅ What's actually recommended?

Guidelines
GuidelineAmountWho says
Carbs as % of calories 45–65% Health Canada (Dietary Reference Intakes)
Carbs on a 2,000-cal day 225–325 g/day (1.6–2.3 kg/week) Calculated from the 45–65% range
Minimum carbs (RDA) 130 g/day (~0.9 kg/week) Health Canada (Dietary Reference Intakes)
Fibre 25 g/day women, 38 g/day men (ages 19–50) Health Canada (Dietary Reference Intakes)

The honest takeaway: our example week lands inside the recommended range for a 2,000-calorie day. Desi food isn't "too many carbs" by default. But if you're cutting to lose fat (eating less than 2,000), the same roti-and-rice plate quickly becomes mostly carbs and not much protein. That's what the swaps below fix.

❌ Myth vs Fact

Myth

"Just switch to atta or millet and you're fine."

Fact

In the ICMR–INDIAB data, swapping refined grains for whole wheat or millet without eating fewer carbs overall wasn't linked to lower diabetes or belly-fat risk. Swapping some carbs for protein (plant, dairy, egg or fish) was. Portion size and protein matter more than the flour.

💪 Recipe: 5-ingredient tandoori-style chicken

Ingredients

  • 500 g chicken breast
  • 200 g nonfat Greek yogurt
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Ginger-garlic paste, lemon and tandoori masala

Steps

  1. Marinate for 1 hour or more.
  2. Bake at 220°C/425°F for 18–22 minutes until it reaches 74°C inside.

  • 2 g carbs
